How it works

The globe shows the navigation satellites above your position. Their orbits come from CelesTrak and are propagated with SGP4/SDP4, the standard model for these element sets. The distance to each satellite is accurate to about ±3 km plus 2 km per day of element age.

Browsers cannot read a phone's GNSS chip. The satellites a receiver would track and use are predicted from their orbits, using the strongest signals at least 10° above the horizon. To see the satellites your phone actually uses, with raw signal travel times, install the Android app.

The ground error combines three views. Reported is your browser's accuracy figure. Predicted is the fix geometry (DOP) times a single-frequency error budget. Measured is the scatter of your recent fixes, which is meaningful while you stand still.

Without location access, or when orbit data can't be downloaded, the page simulates the sky from a place you pick and labels it Simulated.
